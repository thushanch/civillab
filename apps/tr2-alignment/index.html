What am I seeing?
  • Stopping sight distance is the ground covered while the driver reacts plus the distance to brake to a stop. At 100 km/h the braking part is more than half of the total, and a downgrade makes it longer.
  • The minimum radius balances the outward pull of the curve against superelevation and tyre friction. Doubling the speed needs four times the radius.
  • On the inside of a curve a wall, cutting or hedge blocks the view. The sight line is a chord, and the middle ordinate M is how far back the obstruction must sit.
  • Over a crest the road itself hides the object. A longer vertical curve flattens the hump until the eye at 1.08 m sees a 0.6 m object a full stopping distance ahead.
  • In a sag the problem is at night. The headlight beam climbs at one degree, and the curve must be long enough for it to light the road a stopping distance ahead.
