What am I seeing?
  • Classification comes first, because it decides which formula is even allowed. A class 1 or 2 section can reach its plastic moment, a class 3 only its elastic one, and a class 4 buckles locally before either.
  • The c/t ratios are measured on the drawn section. Thin outstands and slender webs push the class up, and so does axial compression in the web.
  • A strut fails by buckling long before it squashes. The buckling curves turn slenderness into the reduction factor χ, and an I section always buckles first about its weak axis.
  • A beam with an unrestrained compression flange twists sideways. Add lateral restraints and the buckling length shrinks, so the moment resistance climbs back towards the plastic moment.
  • Axial load and moment together use up the member twice over. The interaction checks add the two fractions with k factors that allow for the second order effect.
