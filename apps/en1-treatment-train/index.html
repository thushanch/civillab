What am I seeing?
  • Each unit does one job. Screens catch the coarse debris, coagulation turns fine particles into floc, settling removes most of the floc, the filter catches what is left, and chlorine kills what got through.
  • Coagulation removes nothing on its own, yet switch it off and both settling and filtration fall apart, because the fine clay and the organisms are too small to settle or be strained out alone.
  • Disinfection depends on clarity. Particles shield bacteria from chlorine, which is why the water must be at or below 1 NTU before it reaches the contact tank.
  • A clean reservoir can skip the settling tank and filter directly. Monsoon river water cannot, the filter would clog within hours.
  • The removal values are typical teaching figures from the textbooks named in the footer. A real plant proves its performance by jar tests and monitoring.
