What am I seeing?
  • Each particle drifts across the tank with the water and sinks at its own settling velocity, so it travels in a straight sloping line. If that line reaches the floor before the outlet, the particle is caught.
  • Stokes' law makes the settling velocity grow with the square of the diameter. Halve the size and the particle sinks four times slower, which is why fine silt and clay need coagulation.
  • The overflow rate, flow divided by plan area, is the settling velocity the tank can just catch from the surface. Depth drops out completely for an ideal tank.
  • Warm water is less viscous, so the same particle settles faster in the tropics than in a cold country.
  • Stokes only holds while the Reynolds number stays below one. Coarse sand falls fast enough to break that, and the app switches to the transitional drag law.
