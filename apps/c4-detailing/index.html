What am I seeing?
  • A bar can only deliver its yield force if the concrete around it can grip it for long enough. The anchorage length is that grip, the bar force divided by the bond stress times the bar perimeter.
  • Bond is weaker near the top of a deep pour, where water and air rise and collect under the bars. That is the poor bond condition, and it needs 1/0.7 times the length.
  • The α factors reward good detailing. More cover, a bend, welded or confining bars and transverse pressure all shorten the bar, but never below 70 per cent for α2 α3 α5 together.
  • Cover does three jobs at once. It lets the bar grip, keeps chlorides and carbonation away from the steel for the design life, and insulates the bar in a fire. The largest need wins.
  • The clear gap must let the largest stone pass between the bars, otherwise the concrete cannot surround them and the grip assumed above never forms.
