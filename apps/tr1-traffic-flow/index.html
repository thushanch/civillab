What am I seeing?
  • The three plots are one relationship seen three ways, because flow is always density times speed. Drag the point on any of them and it moves on the other two.
  • Flow rises as cars join the road, peaks at capacity, then falls as they get in each other's way. Under Greenshields the peak sits at half the jam density and half the free flow speed.
  • Every flow below capacity can happen two ways, light and fast or dense and slow. The right half of the flow curve is congestion, and the road carries less there, not more.
  • When two traffic states meet, the boundary between them moves at the slope of the chord joining them on the flow curve. A negative slope sends the queue tail backwards up the road.
  • Greenberg suits dense traffic and Underwood light traffic. Comparing the three shows how much the capacity depends on the model chosen.
