What am I seeing?
  • Below an outfall bacteria eat the waste and use up oxygen, while the surface puts oxygen back. The sag is the race between the two.
  • At first deoxygenation wins and the oxygen falls. At the critical point the two rates are equal, and after it the river slowly recovers.
  • A fast, shallow river reaerates quickly, so the same load sags far less than in a slow, deep one.
  • Warm water holds less oxygen and speeds up the bacteria, so the worst sags come in the dry, hot season at low flow.
  • The allowed load is the largest waste BOD that keeps the minimum oxygen at the standard. It shows why treatment before discharge matters.
