What am I seeing?
  • A one way slab bends like a wide beam. Once the long side passes twice the short side, a two way panel behaves the same way, because the short direction is far stiffer.
  • In a two way panel both directions carry load. The short span takes the larger share, so its bars sit in the outer layer where the lever arm is longest.
  • Continuous edges attract hogging moment and need top steel. A discontinuous edge releases that moment back into the span, which is why corner panels get the biggest coefficients.
  • Punching is a cone of concrete pushed through the slab by the column. The check is made on a perimeter at 2d in EN 1992-1-1, with rounded corners, and at 1.5d with square corners in BS 8110.
  • Edge and corner columns lose part of their perimeter and carry an unbalanced moment, so β grows from 1.15 to 1.4 and 1.5.
