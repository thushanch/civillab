What am I seeing?
  • Masonry is strong in compression and weak everywhere else. A load that sits off centre bends the wall, and the reduction factor Φ takes away the part of the strength that the bending uses up.
  • A tall thin wall bows at mid height, so the eccentricity grows there. That is why Φ at mid height usually governs and why EC6 caps hef/tef at 27.
  • Concrete floors bearing on the wall hold its top and bottom, so the effective height drops to 0.75h. Returns or piers at the ends shorten it again.
  • Under wind a panel supported on four edges cracks along yield lines from its corners. It is stronger spanning across the bed joints than along them, and the orthogonal ratio μ decides the shape of the cracks.
  • Wedge the same wall tightly between rigid supports and it carries lateral load as an arch instead of in bending, often several times more.
